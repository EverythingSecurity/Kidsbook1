Hi! I'm Detective Dot! 🔍

Dot says: "Sometimes people use sneaky tricks to win an argument. I call them Logic Monsters! I will help you spot them."
A shield is a kind sentence that stops a trick. Use it to find out what is true, never to be rude.

Mission 1

Give a Good Reason 🧱

Dot says: "A good answer has a reason. The reason starts with because."
Answer:
"Barnaby has fur."
Reason:
"Because Barnaby is a dog, and dogs have fur."
Play: Say "I want ice cream." Now give a because. Which reason is strong?
  • "Because it is a hot day." ✔
  • "Because everyone who says no is silly!" ✘ (that is a name, not a reason)

Mission 2

Mean Words and Silly Changes 🐉

Mud-Slinging Dragon
Mud Dragon says mean things about YOU, not about the idea.
Shield: "That's mean. Let's talk about my idea."
Example: "Don't listen to Leo. He is bad at spelling!"
Smelly Fish Distractor
Smelly Fish changes the subject.
Shield: "Fish are fun, but let's get back to my question."
Example: "Did you clean your room?" "Did you see the new puppy?"
Cardboard Dummy
Cardboard Dummy twists what you said into something silly.
Shield: "I never said that. I said…"
Example: "Let's add salad." "You want to ban pizza?!"
Mirror-Mirror Monster
Mirror Monster says "You too!" instead of answering.
Shield: "Maybe I did too, but now we are talking about the shoes."
Example: "You left muddy shoes!" "You left your bag!"

Mission 3

Only Two Choices? Scary Stories? 🐙

Either-Or Octopus
Either-Or Octopus says there are only 2 choices. There are almost always more!
Shield: "Or we could do something in the middle!"
Example: "Either you play soccer with us, or you hate us!"
Avalanche Slide
Scary Slide says one small thing will lead to a giant disaster.
Shield: "Is that really going to happen? Let's check."
Example: "If we get 5 minutes of homework, soon we'll never sleep again!"

Mission 4

Everybody, Luck and Threats 🐓

Crowd Mob Bandwagon
Crowd Monster says "everybody does it, so it's right!"
Shield: "Lots of people can be wrong. What is the reason?"
Example: "Everybody plays this game, so it's the best!"
Rooster-Sunrise Rooster
Rooster says "B came after A, so A made B happen!" But the rooster crows and the sun rises anyway.
Shield: "Maybe it was studying, not the socks."
Example: "I wore lucky socks and got an A. The socks did it!"
Playground Bully Rod
Bully Stick scares you instead of giving a reason.
Shield: "I will listen to reasons, not threats."
Example: "Say my comic is cooler or you can't sit with us!"

Mission 5

Words With Two Meanings 🦎

Word Chameleon
Word Chameleon uses a word that changes meaning in the middle of a sentence.
Shield: "Which one do you mean?"
Example: "Feathers are light. Dark is the opposite of light. So feathers can't be dark!"
(First "light" means "not heavy". Second means "not dark".)

🔑 Answer Key and Note for Grown-ups

  • Mission 1: (1) A is the strong reason. B is only an opinion and C is a name-calling. (2) Open-ended: check there is a real reason after "because". (3) Open-ended.
  • Mission 2: (1) Dragon, Fish, Dummy, Mirror. (2) Dragon → "Let's talk about my idea." Fish → "Let's get back to my question." Dummy → "I never said that."
  • Mission 3: Open-ended. For example: "Study for one hour on Saturday" or "Make flash cards and ask for help."
  • Mission 4: Case 1: No. It is the Rooster (one thing after another is not the same as one thing causing another). Case 2: Crowd. Case 3: Bully Stick. The rooster does not make the sun rise.
  • Mission 5: The word "light" changes meaning. Bat: the animal and the baseball bat.
  • Tournament: (1) Mud Dragon, (2) Octopus, (3) Crowd, (4) Smelly Fish, (5) Bully Stick. The Be the Judge answers are open-ended.
For parents and teachers: This is a gentle first look at common reasoning mistakes. The "proper" names are: Mud Dragon = Ad Hominem, Smelly Fish = Red Herring, Dummy = Straw Man, Mirror = Tu Quoque, Octopus = False Dilemma, Scary Slide = Slippery Slope, Crowd = Appeal to Numbers, Rooster = Post Hoc, Bully Stick = Appeal to Force, Chameleon = Equivocation. Children do not need these names yet. Practise the shields at home with "Is that a reason, or a trick?" The idea that logic is a shield and a sword comes from Madsen Pirie's How to Win Every Argument.
