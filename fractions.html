Chapter 1

Slicing the Pizza: What is a Fraction? 🍕

A warm, freshly baked pizza is one whole pizza. To share it, you cut it into equal pieces. The word fraction comes from the Latin word fractus, which means "broken" or "split into equal parts". A fraction is a number that tells us how many equal parts of a whole we are talking about.

The Denominator (bottom)
From the Latin word for "the namer". It tells how many equal parts make up one whole.
Rule: it can never be zero, because you can't split something into zero pieces!
The Numerator (top)
From the Latin word for "the counter". It tells how many of those parts we have.
Pizza example: 4 slices, you eat 3. You ate 34, and 14 is left in the box!

Proper fractions
The gentle giants
Smaller than one whole. The top is smaller than the bottom.
14   23   49
Improper fractions
The top-heavy superheroes
One whole or more. The top is bigger than or equal to the bottom.
54   94   33
Mixed numbers
The team players
A whole number and a proper fraction side by side.
234 says "two and three-quarters"

See it! Five-fourths is more than one pizza

Pepper: "Think of an acrobat balancing a giant bear on top: the top is bigger than the bottom! That's top-heavy."

Turn top-heavy into mixed: the conversion recipe

  1. Divide the top number by the bottom number.
  2. The quotient (how many full times it fits) is the whole number.
  3. The remainder (what's left) is the new top number.
  4. The bottom number stays exactly the same!

Example A: 94. 9 ÷ 4 = 2 remainder 1 (because 4 × 2 = 8 and 9 − 8 = 1). So 94 = 214, "two and one-quarter".

Example B: 2415. 24 ÷ 15 = 1 remainder 9 (because 15 × 1 = 15 and 24 − 15 = 9). So 2415 = 1915.

Chapter 3

Fraction Twins and Magic Walls 🧱

Two different fractions can mean exactly the same amount of chocolate! When fractions look different but have the same value, they are equivalent fractions, or fraction twins.

 1/2
 2/4
 4/8
12 = 24 = 48   Same chocolate!
The Golden Rule: Whatever you multiply (or divide) the top by, you must do the exact same thing to the bottom!
12 → ×2 → 24    34 → ×2 → 68    35 → ×3 → 915

The Fraction Wall

Look straight up and down the wall: 12 lines up with 24, 36 and 48!
Unit fractions are fractions with a top number of 1: 12, 13, 15, 18... Every fraction is built from them. 34 is just three copies of 14: 14 + 14 + 14.
Simplifying means making the numbers as small as possible without changing the value. Find a number that divides both top and bottom, then divide. When only 1 divides both, it's in its simplest form (irreducible).

The Four Superhero Powers 🦸

Now you can ADD, SUBTRACT, MULTIPLY and DIVIDE fractions!

Power 1: Adding

Same bottom numbers? Easy peasy! Add the tops. Keep the bottom.

+ =
48 + 38 = 78

Another: 1315 + 1115 = 2415 = 1915 = 135 (simplified by dividing by 3).

Different bottom numbers? Rename them as twins with the same bottom number first!

Example: 14 + 13
1. A common bottom for 4 and 3 is 4 × 3 = 12.
2. 14 = 312 and 13 = 412.
3. Add the tops: 312 + 412 = 712.

Power 2: Subtracting

Same idea: subtract the tops and keep the bottom.

35 − 15 = 25    1720 − 1220 = 520 = 14

Example: 34 − 512
12 is already a multiple of 4. 34 = 912. Then 912 − 512 = 412 = 13.

Power 3: Multiplying (just go straight across!)

Top × top, bottom × bottom. No common bottoms needed!
12 × 34 = 38    24 × 34 = 616 = 38
Whole number? Write it over 1. Example: 3 × 34 = 31 × 34 = 94 = 214

Power 4: Dividing: Keep, Switch, Flip!

Example A: 12 ÷ 34. Keep 12, switch to ×, flip 34 to 43: 12 × 43 = 46 = 23.

Example B: 3 ÷ 12. How many halves are in 3 whole sandwiches? 31 × 21 = 61 = 6.

Cut 3 sandwiches in half. Count the halves: 6!

Example C: 14 ÷ 2. 14 × 12 = 18. Split a quarter-slice between 2 kids and each gets an eighth.

Chapter 5

Secret History: The Egyptian Pizza Party 🏺

More than 3,000 years ago, ancient Egyptian scribes were already math geniuses! A famous scroll, the Rhind Mathematical Papyrus, was written by a scribe named Ahmes around 1650 BC. It shows that the Egyptians wrote almost all their fractions as sums of different unit fractions (fractions with a 1 on top). Today these are called Egyptian fractions.

The 5-pizza puzzle

Pharaoh's builders want to share 5 pizzas equally among 8 hungry diners. Each person should get 58 of a pizza. But cutting every pizza into 8 slices means 40 small cuts, and everyone ends up with 5 messy crumbs!

The clever Egyptian way: 58 = 12 + 18

Check it with your new powers: 12 = 48, and 48 + 18 = 58. It works!
