Mission 1

Name It to Tame It 🦉


Amy sounds the alarm when she feels danger: "Fight! Run! Freeze!" When Amy is loud, it is hard to think.

Pete is wise. He thinks, plans and solves problems. He wakes up when you say the word for your feeling.
Name it to tame it! When you say the exact word, like "I feel disappointed," Amy gets quieter and Pete can help. Scientists have found that putting feelings into words can make big feelings a bit smaller.

🎮 Game: The One-Word Circle

Mission 5

Calming the Storm 🌊

When a feeling reaches 7 or more, thinking gets hard. You can't argue the storm away, but you can breathe it down.

Belly Breathing: 4 in, 5 out

Put a hand on your belly. Smell the cookie slowly (count 4). Blow out the candle even more slowly (count 5). Do it 4 times. A long, slow breath out helps your body settle down.

Mission 6

Thought Clouds ☁️

Pete says: "Thoughts are like clouds. They float by. But a thought is not always a fact! When we are upset, our mind can play tricks: 'Everyone hates me!' or 'I can't do anything right!'"
What we think changes how we feel, and how we feel changes what we do. A kinder, more true thought can change both.

🔑 Answer Key and Note for Grown-ups

  • Mission 1: (1) frustrated, nervous, delighted (more exact words tell more). (2) and (3) are personal.
  • Mission 2: Colors and numbers are personal. There are no wrong numbers. A pencil taken might be 3 to 6, dropped ice cream 4 to 7, loud thunder 5 to 9. Different children will differ.
  • Mission 3: (1) lonely, frustrated, startled, proud. (2) Sad family (e.g. disappointed, left out, lonely). (3) open-ended.
  • Mission 4: Sleepover mix might be excited, nervous, a bit homesick. Leo may feel frustrated or blocked; Maya eager or excited. Sample sentence: "I feel frustrated, so can I finish this part, and then we build together?"
  • Mission 5: Answer to 3 is B.
  • Mission 6: (1) e.g. "We lost today, and we can practice for next time." (2) e.g. "I feel annoyed when someone takes my seat. Next time, I need you to ask first." (3) T, R, T.
For parents and teachers: This book follows the Feelings Explorers course: naming feelings ("name it to tame it"), body awareness, a wider feelings vocabulary, mixed feelings, slow breathing and simple thought-reframing with I-statements. Amy and Pete are playful pictures of the brain's alarm system and its thinking part; real brains are more complicated. Research suggests that finding words for feelings helps people feel calmer. Let your child pass on any activity. The book builds skills and is not a substitute for help from a doctor or counsellor. If a child's feelings seem too big to manage, please talk to a trusted professional.
Progress guide: Early on, children say "good / bad / mad". Next they name families (angry, sad, scared). Later they use exact words (frustrated, discouraged, uneasy), notice body clues early, feel two feelings at once, and use I-statements.
